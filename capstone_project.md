## The Data
I found two data sets from the Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services website at this <a href="https://data.cms.gov/summary-statistics-on-use-and-payments/medicare-medicaid-service-type-reports/cms-program-statistics-medicare-part-d">link</a> for Medicare Part D and

For context, there are four parts to Medicare: <br>
  Part A: Hospital Insurance <br>
  Part B: Medical Insurance <br>
  Part C: Medicare Advantage <br>
  Part D: Drug Insurance <br>

Part A helps to cover inpatient hospital visits, skilled nursing, home health, and hospice care. <br>
Part B helps to cover services from doctors/other health providers, home health (coming from a hospital or skilled nursing visit), outpatient care, durable equipment, and preventative services.<br>
Part C is provided by a private Medicare-approved company and offers a package deal with combining Parts A, B, and D.<br>
Part D helps to cover prescription drugs. 

In creating this project, I wanted to do a deep dive into the different parts of Medicare and answer the following questions:
  1. Is Medicare Part A or Part B used more often?
  2. How do premiums compare between Part A and Part B?
  3. How much of Medicare is actually used and what in price bracket?
  4. How do states compare with Medicare usage in Parts A, B, and D?

## Analysis

### Question 1
Is Medicare Part A or Part B used more often? I created a pivot chart in Excel and created a bar graph to see the difference between the two.

<>

As you can see, Part B far surpassed Part A in 2021 usage. Almost 11 billion people pay for Medicare Part B in the $100-$499 bracket and another 11 billion people paid between $2,000-$4,999. I was surprised at these results because I would think Part A would cost a lot more since that is used for inpatient hospital visits, skilled nursing, home health, and hospice care. But while those visits might cost more, maybe doctors/other health providers, other home health, outpatient care, durable equipment, and preventative services are utilized more often so cost for those adds up.
